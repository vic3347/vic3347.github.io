About

A sociologist of race, institutions, and public life, Victor Thompson brings together comparative research, social psychology, social demography, and rigorous research methods.

Current position

Associate Professor of Sociology and Chair, Department of Sociology, Criminal Justice and Social Work, Rider University, July 2016–present.

Education

Ph.D. and M.A. in Sociology from Stanford University; B.A. in Sociology from the University of Illinois Urbana–Champaign.

Research
questions

How do census categories shape belonging? How do race and social context influence attitudes toward crime and punishment? What can comparative data reveal about identity, inequality, and the state?

  • Race & ethnicity
  • Enumeration practices
  • Crime attitudes
  • Social psychology
  • Social demography
  • Research methods

Global ethnic enumeration

Comparative work on how nations count race, ethnicity, and culture in censuses and other population systems, including the project Global Ethnic Enumeration Practices: Race, Ethnicity, and Culture in the Census, 1965–2024.

Race, crime & punishment

Research on racialized mass incarceration, public attitudes toward crime, the legitimacy of criminal justice institutions, and how social experience colors support for policy.

Radical Right Violence and Racialized Grievance

Grievance and violence in extremist groups.
